## Executive Summary & Core Verdict

The proposed 20-workstream research architecture provides a structured starting point, but in its initial formulation it suffers from **six fundamental blind spots** that would compromise any investment or development thesis:

1. **The Fuel Logistics Blind Spot:** The electricity sector cannot be evaluated in isolation from primary hydrocarbon import logistics. Guinea has no operating oil refinery; all thermal and captive mining generation depends on imported liquid fuels (HFO/LFO/diesel) handled through Conakry port and coastal depots. The catastrophic December 2023 Conakry oil depot explosion proved that liquid fuel logistics is a single point of failure for Guinea's economy, mining operations, and thermal generation fleet.
2. **The Supranational River Basin & Hydrological Reality:** Guinea is the "water tower of West Africa" (*château d'eau de l'Afrique de l'Ouest*). Hydro generation cannot be analyzed as ordinary utility assets. Major dams fall under supranational river basin treaties (OMVG, OMVS, ABN) with statutory multi-country quota allocations, cross-border transmission regimes, and international operating companies. Furthermore, the extreme seasonality between the monsoon (July–October) and the dry season (December–May) creates two completely different power systems each year.
3. **The Mining-Energy Disconnect:** Mining represents >80% of Guinea's export revenue and the overwhelming majority of unconstrained power demand. Mining energy is not a standard industrial load: it is governed by bilateral Mining Conventions (*Conventions Minières*), statutory bauxite-to-alumina refining obligations, and the 600+ km multi-use Simandou infrastructure corridor (Compagnie du Transguinéen - CTG). Treating mining as a generic sub-sector will fatally miscalculate demand.
4. **Utility Insolvency & Offtaker Bankability:** Électricité de Guinée (EDG) operates under severe financial distress, technical/commercial losses, low cash collection, and massive tariff subsidies. Evaluating utility-scale IPPs on the assumption of a sovereign-backed standard utility PPA without deep financial modeling of EDG's circular debt and DFI de-risking mechanisms is commercially fatal.
5. **Macro-Financial & Currency Architecture:** Guinea operates the Guinean Franc (GNF) under the Banque Centrale de la République de Guinée (BCRG). It is **not** part of the CFA Franc zone (WAEMU/UEMOA). There is no French Treasury convertibility guarantee. PPA dollarization, FX scarcity, convertibility restrictions, and offshore debt service escrow accounts are existential hurdles for international project finance.
6. **False Linear Forecasting:** Simple 5-year incremental demand projections (2027–2030, 2030–2035) fail in Guinea because mining and industrial demand is **lumpy and binary**. A single mandated alumina refinery adds 200–400 MW of electrical baseload and vast thermal steam demand overnight; if delayed, demand growth remains low. The architecture must adopt a **scenario-based and seasonal-dispatch** model.

## Section A: Missing Research Domains

The proposed 20-workstream architecture omits several macro-level domains that directly determine project viability:

### Domain 21: Fuel Supply Chains, Primary Hydrocarbon Logistics & Landed Cost Dynamics
* **Rationale:** Thermal generation (utility peaking/baseload and off-grid mining captive power) is governed by the landed, delivered cost of Heavy Fuel Oil (HFO), Light Fuel Oil (LFO), and diesel. 
* **Key Scope:** Conakry port oil terminal capacity, coastal storage infrastructure, post-2023 depot reconstruction status, coastal barging (Conakry to Kamsar, Boffa, Katougouma, Dapilon), inland tanker trucking routes, fuel taxation/subsidies, and the feasibility/cost of planned Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) import solutions (e.g., FSRU studies at Kamsar or Conakry).

### Domain 22: Hydrological Governance, Cascade Dispatch & Supranational River Basin Treaties
* **Rationale:** Hydro assets cannot be analyzed purely by installed MW. Guinea's rivers supply multi-country basins. 
* **Key Scope:** Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du fleuve Gambie (OMVG), Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du fleuve Sénégal (OMVS), and Autorité du Bassin du Niger (ABN). Treaties, multi-country water allocations, asset-holding companies (e.g., SOGEM, SOGES), cascade hydraulic dispatch rules (e.g., upstream multi-year reservoir storage at Souapiti versus downstream run-of-river plants at Kaléta and Garafiri on the Konkouré basin), and seasonal flow variation.

### Domain 23: Offtaker Financial Solvency, Tariff Subsidies, Cost Recovery & Circular Debt
* **Rationale:** An IPP's bankability depends entirely on offtaker cash flow. 
* **Key Scope:** Balance sheet analysis of EDG; tariff structures (social vs. commercial vs. industrial); government operational subsidies; public administration arrears; cash collection efficiency; billing metering losses; circular debt between EDG, fuel suppliers, private rental power operators (e.g., Karpowership / floating power plants), and transmission asset owners.

### Domain 24: Macro-Financial Framework, Currency (GNF) Convertibility & FX Repatriation
* **Rationale:** Guinea's monetary independence from the CFA zone creates currency mismatch risks.
* **Key Scope:** Banque Centrale de la République de Guinée (BCRG) foreign exchange regulations; statutory surrender requirements for export earnings; bilateral PPA currency indexing (USD/EUR vs. GNF); availability of foreign exchange for debt service and dividend repatriation; central bank liquidity; offshore escrow mechanisms and political risk insurance (MIGA, ATIDI).

### Domain 25: Simandou Megaproject Energy & Infrastructure Ecosystem
* **Rationale:** The Simandou iron ore deposit (Blocks 1 & 2 Winning Consortium Simandou; Blocks 3 & 4 Rio Tinto Simfer / Baowu) represents one of the largest infrastructure undertakings in Africa.
* **Key Scope:** Dedicated mine-site power generation; power requirements for the 600+ km Compagnie du Transguinéen (CTG) heavy-haul railway; deepwater port facilities at Moribaya (Forécariah); captive hybrid/thermal systems vs. national grid interconnection; potential spillover power to surrounding communities (Nzérékoré, Beyla, Kérouané, Forécariah).

### Domain 26: Land Tenure, Customary Rights, Community Resettlement & ESIA Safeguards
* **Rationale:** Land acquisition is a primary cause of project delay and cancellation in West Africa.
* **Key Scope:** Customary vs. statutory land rights (*Code Foncier et Domanial*); expropriation and compensation frameworks; Resettlement Action Plan (RAP) precedents; IFC Performance Standards compliance (PS5 Land Acquisition, PS6 Biodiversity); Western Chimpanzee (*Pan troglodytes verus*) habitat corridors; community protest risks in mining/energy regions (Boké, Fria, Kindia).

### Domain 27: Decentralized Electrification, Mini-Grids & Productive Uses
* **Rationale:** Grid extension across Guinea's mountainous terrain (Fouta Djallon, Forest Region) is economically unviable for dispersed populations.
* **Key Scope:** Agence Guinéenne d'Électrification Rurale (AGER) framework; mini-grid concession licensing; tariff equalization models; anchor-load business models (mini-grids anchored by telecom towers, agricultural processing, or artisanal mining sites).

├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

1. **Generation:**
   * What are the precise contractual terms, capacity charges, and fuel pass-through costs of existing or historical emergency/rental power contracts (e.g., Karpowership, Aggreko)?
   * How is water dispatched between upstream storage (Souapiti) and downstream plants (Kaléta, Garafiri)? Who determines seasonal dispatch priority: EDG, the Ministry, or sovereign debt service covenants?
   * What are the actual operating heat rates, fuel types (HFO 180 cSt vs. 380 cSt vs. Gasoil), and ambient-temperature derating factors of EDG's thermal units (Tombo, Kipé)?

2. **Transmission & System Operation:**
   * What are the transformer MVA ratings and N-1 contingencies at key bulk supply substations (Maneah, Matoto, Boké, Mamou, Labé)?
   * Is the 225 kV national backbone suffering from severe reactive power deficits, voltage collapse, or excessive line charging during light-load conditions? Are shunt reactors or STATCOMs installed?
   * What is the operational capability of the National Dispatch Centre (*Centre National de Conduite* - CNC)? Does it have full SCADA visibility over IPPs and mining tie-lines, or is dispatch manual via telephone?

3. **Mining & Industrial Energy:**
   * What is the actual delivered cost of fuel (USD/tonne HFO and USD/m³ diesel) at remote mine sites after accounting for port fees, transport markups, security escort, and fuel theft?
   * For planned alumina refineries (Bayer process), what is the ratio of high-pressure steam required for bauxite digestion versus electrical power for calcination and grinding? Can renewable power alone satisfy refinery demand without massive thermal boiler plants?
   * What are the enforcement mechanisms and penalties under the Guinean Mining Code and individual *Conventions Minières* regarding the obligation to build local alumina refineries?

4. **WAPP & Cross-Border Trading:**
   * What are the actual transmission wheeling tariffs charged by TRANSCO CLSG and OMVG, and is EDG in financial arrears on wheeling fees or power purchases from CI-ENERGIES?
   * What technical synchronization barriers exist? Can Guinea's grid operate synchronously with the WAPP interconnected system, or does it require islanded radial operation due to frequency instability?
   * What are the physical import/export constraints at border substations (e.g., Linsan, Nzérékoré)?

## Section C: Guinea-Specific Institutions & Primary Sources to Target

The research must target specific statutory bodies, public entities, and legal instruments without asserting unverified facts:

### 1. Government Ministries & Planning Authorities
* **Ministère de l'Énergie, de l'Hydraulique et des Hydrocarbures (MEHH):** *Lettre de Politique de Développement du Secteur de l'Énergie (LPDSE)*, national electrification master plans, ministerial decrees.

* **Ministère du Plan et de la Coopération Internationale (MPCI):** National development strategies (*PNDES*).
* **Ministère de l'Environnement et du Développement Durable (MEDD):** Environmental assessment bureau (*BGACE*), Environmental and Social Impact Assessment (ESIA) certificates.

### 2. State Utilities, Agencies & Commercial Vehicles
* **Électricité de Guinée (EDG):** Annual technical and financial reports, tariff schedules (*Grille Tarifaire*), grid connection technical requirements, system outage logs.
* **Agence Guinéenne d'Électrification Rurale (AGER):** Rural electrification master plans, mini-grid concession tenders, off-grid technical standards.
* **Société Nationale des Pétroles (SONAP):** Hydrocarbon import statistics, coastal depot infrastructure plans, fuel supply agreements.
* **Société Guinéenne du Patrimoine Minier (SOGUIPAMI):** State equity participation in mining projects and associated infrastructure.
* **Agence Nationale d'Aménagement des Infrastructures Minières (ANAIM):** State-owned mining infrastructure management (ports, railway concessions, common-user power lines).
* **Compagnie du Transguinéen (CTG):** Joint venture operating the Simandou rail and port infrastructure.

### 3. Regulatory & Oversight Bodies
* **Sectoral Electricity & Water Regulator:** Legal status, decrees, and published tariff determinations of the regulatory authority (verify current operational status of the regulatory body, formerly designated under *AER* / *ARPT*).
* **Autorité de Régulation des Marchés Publics (ARMP):** Independent public procurement audit reports, concession awards, dispute decisions.
* **Cour des Comptes:** Official audit reports on EDG subsidies, treasury transfers, and public enterprise debts.

### 4. Supranational River Basin & Regional Power Bodies

* **Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du fleuve Sénégal (OMVS):** High Commission reports, Koukoutamba dam studies, SOGEM transmission data.
* **Autorité du Bassin du Niger (ABN):** Fomi dam studies and regional water-sharing compacts.
* **West African Power Pool (WAPP) Secretariat & ICC (Cotonou):** WAPP Master Plans, regional dispatch protocols, transmission service agreements.
* **TRANSCO CLSG:** Regional transmission operator annual reports, operational bulletins, wheeling tariff filings.
* **ERERA (ECOWAS Regional Electricity Regulatory Authority):** Regional market rules, dispute resolutions, cross-border tariff benchmarks.

### 5. Multilateral & DFI Project Documentation
* **World Bank / IDA / IFC:** Project Appraisal Documents (PAD), Implementation Status & Results Reports (ISR), and Project Completion Reports (ICR) for:
  * Guinea Power Sector Recovery Project (*PAREC*)
  * Guinea Electricity Sector Efficiency Project (*PAESE*)
  * WAPP Interconnection Projects (CLSG, OMVG)

## Section F: Power-System Reconstruction Architecture

To eliminate reliance on headline capacity numbers and build an engineering-grade model:

```
Nameplate Capacity (MW)
   │  [-] Planned & Forced Outages (EAF Derating)
   ▼
Available Capacity (MW)
   │  [-] Low-Water Hydrological Derating (Seasonal Run-of-River Drop)
   │  [-] Ambient Temperature Thermal Derating (Tombo/Kipé)
   ▼
Dependable Firm Capacity (MW)
   │  [-] Transmission Bottlenecks & Line Thermal Limits
   │  [-] Substation Transformer MVA Overloads
   ▼
Dispatched Generation (MW / GWh)
   │  [-] Transmission Technical Losses (HV)
   │  [-] Distribution Technical Losses (MV/LV)
   ▼
Delivered Electricity (GWh)
   │  [-] Commercial Losses (Theft, Unmetered, Billing Failure)
   ▼
Billed & Collected Electricity (Revenue Base)
```

### Specific Reconstruction Requirements:
1. **Single-Line Diagram (SLD) Topology Mapping:** Reconstruct the complete transmission network topology down to 60 kV/33 kV substations.
2. **Seasonal Capacity De-rating Model:** Quantify the dry-season derating factor for every hydro plant. A 450 MW hydro plant that drops to 80 MW dependable capacity during April must be recorded with both values, not an annual average.
3. **Spinning Reserve & Inertia Analysis:** Evaluate system inertia ($H$) during high-solar or high-hydro dispatch. Identify whether the grid has sufficient synchronous inertia or whether grid-forming (GFM) inverters and BESS fast frequency response (FFR) are technically mandatory for grid survival.
4. **Loss Partitioning Audit:** Isolate transmission losses (typically 4–8%), distribution technical losses (typically 8–15%), and non-technical/commercial losses (often 20–35% in stressed West African utilities).

## Section G: Mining & Industrial Energy Due-Diligence Protocol

Because mining energy demand dominates the country's balance, each major mining asset must be investigated using a site-level protocol:

### Site-Level Intelligence Fields:
1. **Operator & Shareholding:** Concession name, operating entity, ultimate parent companies (e.g., Baowu, Chinalco, Rio Tinto, Winning Consortium, Dadco, EGA).
2. **Mining Type & Processing Depth:** Run-of-mine (ROM) bauxite extraction, crushing, washing, rail haulage vs. Bayer process alumina refining vs. gold milling/cyanidation.
3. **Current Power Source:** Captive thermal capacity (MW), number/make of engines (e.g., Wärtsilä, Caterpillar, Bergen), fuel grade (HFO 380 cSt, HFO 180 cSt, Diesel), delivered fuel price ($/m³ or $/tonne).
4. **Estimated Electrical & Thermal Demand:** Peak load (MW), average continuous load (MW), daily load factor, annual consumption (GWh/year), and process steam demand (tonnes/hour, bar pressure, °C).
5. **Statutory Refining Mandates:** Specific commitments in the bilateral *Convention Minière* regarding refinery construction, statutory COD deadlines, and regulatory status.
6. **Decarbonization Pressures & Offtake Architecture:** Scope 1 & 2 reduction targets, exposure to EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) for exported bauxite/alumina, willingness to sign private B2B PPAs with renewable IPPs.

## Section K: Contradiction Risks & Normalization Protocol

Anticipating discrepancies between sources prevents flawed conclusions:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      CONTRADICTION TAXONOMY & RULES                         │
├──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Metric Mismatch          │ Normalization Rule                               │
├──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Solar Capacity           │ Mandate explicit MW-DC and MW-AC recording.      │
│ Hydro Capacity           │ Record Nameplate MW AND Dry-Season Firm MW.      │
│ Electrification Rate     │ Distinguish Urban Grid vs Rural SHS/Mini-grid.   │
│ Transmission Length      │ Separate Constructed km from Energized km.       │
│ Project Stage            │ Disregard MoUs; require signed financial close.  │
│ Mining Energy Demand     │ Separate Operating Base from Speculative Refine. │
└──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Installed vs. Dependable Capacity:**
   * *Cause:* Official releases cite nameplate MW; technical DFI reports cite dry-season available MW.
   * *Resolution:* Record both with explicit operational season labels. Never use nameplate capacity for dry-season energy balancing.
2. **Electrification / Access Rates:**
   * *Cause:* EDG counts formal grid connections; World Bank models multi-tier access (MTF); survey data includes pico-solar home systems.
   * *Resolution:* Disaggregate by Tier 1–2 (SHS) versus Tier 3–5 (Grid/Mini-grid), and disaggregate Conakry/Urban from Rural.
3. **Transmission Line Status:**
   * *Cause:* Ministry press releases announce line "completion" when towers are strung; utility reports classify line as "non-operational" pending substation commissioning or synchronization.
   * *Resolution:* Require substations energization proof before classifying lines as operational.
4. **Project Pipeline Double-Counting:**
   * *Cause:* Identical dam sites or solar concessions re-announced under different project names by successive ministerial administrations or changing developer consortia.
   * *Resolution:* Cross-reference geographic coordinates, river coordinates, and capacity ratings to eliminate duplicate entries.

## Section L: Commercially Valuable "Hidden" Information

High-value intelligence lies outside standard public reports:

1. **True Delivered Cost of Fuel at Mines:** Public reports quote Conakry wholesale fuel prices. True landed mine-gate cost includes transport demurrage, private security escort, road condition surcharges, and informal shrinkage (theft), often resulting in delivered costs 30–50% above coastal retail ($0.25–$0.38/kWh equivalent thermal generation cost).
2. **Article 130 Infrastructure Sharing Precedents:** Under the Guinean Mining Code, infrastructure (ports, rail, power lines) built by mining concessionaires must provide third-party open access where spare capacity exists. Tracking whether ANAIM or private miners have successfully negotiated open-access power evacuation agreements reveals IPP connection options.
3. **Private Bilateral PPA Legality:** Can an IPP legally sell power directly to a mining company over a private transmission line without EDG intermediation? The legal interpretation of the *Code de l'Électricité* regarding off-grid self-generation vs. private commercial sale determines whether utility-risk-free B2B projects are legally viable.
4. **Wet-Season Hydro Spillage & Curtailment:** During the peak monsoon (August–October), do Souapiti and Kaléta spill water without generating due to transmission bottlenecks or lack of domestic load? This unutilized energy represents a zero-marginal-cost opportunity for seasonal industrial loads or WAPP exports.

## Section P: Opportunity Screening & Multi-Criteria Ranking Algorithm

Candidate opportunities must be evaluated across five weighted dimensions using an objective, verifiable scoring model:

```
Opportunity Score (0 - 100) = 
    0.25 * [Offtaker Bankability & Payment Security]
  + 0.20 * [Technical & Evacuation Feasibility]
  + 0.20 * [Regulatory, Permitting & Land Pathway]
  + 0.20 * [Commercial, Financial & FX Viability]
  + 0.15 * [Alendei Strategic Orchestration Value]
```

### Dimension Criteria & Scoring Breakdown:
1. **Offtaker Bankability & Payment Security (Weight: 25%)**
   * *100 pts:* Private investment-grade international mining company, USD-denominated contract, offshore escrow.
   * *50 pts:* EDG utility offtake with full sovereign guarantee backed by active World Bank IDA PRG or MIGA policy.
   * *10 pts:* Unsecured EDG utility PPA with no multilateral credit enhancement.
2. **Technical & Evacuation Feasibility (Weight: 20%)**
   * *100 pts:* Direct on-site generation (behind-the-meter) or <10 km to robust 225 kV substation with documented spare MVA capacity.
   * *50 pts:* 10–50 km grid connection requiring line extension; substation upgrade required.
   * *0 pts:* Evacuation constrained by weak 110 kV line or unbuilt WAPP segment.
3. **Regulatory, Permitting & Land Pathway (Weight: 20%)**
   * *100 pts:* Industrial/mining concession land with pre-cleared environmental permits; clear private captive legal status.
   * *50 pts:* Rural statutory land with low-density customary claims; standard ESIA Category B.
   * *10 pts:* Critical chimpanzee habitat corridor; complex customary resettlement; disputed land title.
4. **Commercial, Financial & FX Viability (Weight: 20%)**
   * *100 pts:* Tariff allows >15% IRR; project qualifies for DFI senior debt; full FX convertibility mechanism established.
   * *50 pts:* Modest IRR headroom; heavy reliance on local currency (GNF) indexing; concessional finance required.
   * *0 pts:* Commercial viability dependent on unverified government tariff subsidies.
5. **Alendei Strategic Orchestration Value (Weight: 15%)**
   * *100 pts:* Project requires complex multi-party ecosystem integration (e.g., hybridizing solar + BESS + hydro dispatch with mining captive offtaker and Indian/international EPC/OEM coordination).
   * *50 pts:* Standard utility-scale EPC contract where Alendei has no unique structural differentiator.
   * *0 pts:* Speculative megaproject outside private development scope.

### Portfolio Classification Thresholds:
* **Tier 1 — Pursue Immediately:** Score $\ge 80$, Offtaker Bankability $\ge 75$, Land/Permit $\ge 75$.
* **Tier 2 — Develop:** Score $65 - 79$, clear pathway to de-risk offtake or evacuation constraints.
* **Tier 3 — Monitor:** Score $50 - 64$, structurally attractive but blocked by pipeline delays or macro factors.
* **Tier 4 — Do Not Pursue Now:** Score $< 50$ or fatal flaw identified (e.g., uncreditworthy unsecured offtake, severe environmental restriction, legal prohibition).
